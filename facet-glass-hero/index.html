Real-time glass, in five terms.

Iridescence — A thin film on the surface interferes with itself, so the reflected colour shifts with the viewing angle: the green-to-violet sheen that slides across a piece as it tips.

Index of refraction — How strongly light bends as it enters the material. Glass sits around 1.5; higher values pull reflections tighter and make edges read brighter.

Dispersion — Each wavelength refracts by a slightly different amount, splitting white light into thin coloured fringes along bevels and cuts.

Transmission — The share of light that passes through the surface rather than bouncing off it, which lets a shard tint and distort whatever sits behind it.

Roughness — Microscopic surface scatter. Low values keep reflections crisp; higher values soften them into the smoked, satin finish of the faces.
